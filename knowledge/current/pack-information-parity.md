# Pack information parity — decoration decals

Status: **authoritative current decoration visual contract** (supersedes the face-tint grammar in
[`m3-visual-semantics.md`](m3-visual-semantics.md) §3 for single-variant models; that grammar remains
the fallback)

## 1. Why catalog parity was not enough

[`source-raw-parity-audit.md`](source-raw-parity-audit.md) proves that every source RPO key, target and
state maps to a BlockLens capability (37 keys, 323 bindings). It does not prove that a player can read
the same information. The original M3 grammar tinted whole faces by multiplication, and only the faces
the state selected:

| Situation | Source pack | M3 tint grammar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glazed terracotta in a floor (only the top is visible) | arrow on the top face | cue on the `facing` side face, which the floor hides |
| Double slab in a wall | red seam on every side face | cue on top/bottom only, hidden by the rows above and below |
| Log/wood/froglight seen from a side | axis-colored stripe on side faces | cue on the two end faces only |
| Dark or same-hue textures (deepslate, blackstone, yellow terracotta) | saturated line work | multiplicative pale tint, little visible change |
| Bottom vs top slab | frame on the face that meets the block boundary | tint on the opposite (mid-height) face |

The project's own M3 evidence scene shows the first case: its camera looks north at blocks placed with
`facing=north`, so the anvil and glazed-terracotta tints sit on faces the camera cannot see.

## 2. Current decal grammar

Decoration cues are drawn as cut-out decal quads on top of the active texture, lit exactly like the face
under them (block and sky light, directional shade, ambient occlusion), so they darken in darkness as the
source pack's textures do. Each decal is
one of seven original 32×32 glyphs, stored as one 32×224 strip sprite generated by
`scripts/generate_decoration_glyphs.py` (no source pixels), tinted per semantic state:

| Capability | Decal |
| --- | --- |
| Glazed terracotta | Arrow on all six faces, in the source template's per-face rotations: top/bottom and the side faces point in the placer's look direction (opposite `facing`); the `facing` face shows it up and the opposite face down. White with a dark contour. |
| Log, wood, froglight | End faces: inset frame. Side faces: rails parallel to the axis. X red, Y green, Z blue. |
| Slabs | Bottom slab: edge frame on its bottom face. Top slab: edge frame on its top face. Double slab: frame with a centered seam on every side, double frame on top and bottom. Red. |
| Stairs | The source pack's drawing (§5): the back face (toward `facing`, where the step stands) is outlined in green; every other full face is outlined in amber with a green edge where it meets the back face; on full faces the step edges hidden behind the face are dashed; any other surface carries a green line where it meets the back face. The front shows the plain block. |
| Trapdoor | Two hinge stubs on the edge the panel pivots on and a handle on the opposite edge, dark metal, on both panel faces; the two edge faces along the hinge and the handle carry the same marks over their height (§6). |
| Anvil | Arrow on the top face only, pointing in the placer's look direction, white with a dark contour: where the source pack's top-texture arrow points under the vanilla `template_anvil` UVs (§4). Lower steps of the model carry no decal. |
| Beehive, campfire, grindstone, fence gate | Inset outline on the faces the M3 grammar already selects, in its semantic colors (honey full, lit/unlit, mount face, open/closed). |
| Stained glass | No decal; the solid-layer behavior is unchanged. |

Glyph rotation is computed from vertex positions in a fixed viewer frame per face (screen-right ×
screen-up = outward normal), never from model UVs, so resource packs that rotate textures do not change
the cue. The glyph is fitted to each quad's own bounds, so slabs show their actual structure. Stairs and
trapdoors instead draw lines derived from the semantic state alone (`DecorationLines`), in the same
viewer frames, because their information sits at fixed positions of the block's shape rather than on
whole quads.

## 3. Composition and fallback

- Decals are drawn only when the wrapped model is exactly one fixed variant (`SingleVariant`): its
  geometry never changes, so the decal quads are encoded once per (enabled decal cues, glyph sprite),
  grouped by cull face, and replayed per block under the same cull test. A different glyph sprite
  (any atlas change) rebuilds them. Weighted variants, multipart models and other mods' wrappers (for
  example Simplified Terrain's) keep the M3 tint for that capability.
- A capability drawn as a decal is not also tinted. Other cues on the same block (Nether Tweaks, glass
  highlights, resource highlights) compose as before.
- Decals are lifted 1/256 block along the face normal, above the Nether band (0.03/16) and ore border
  (0.02/16) overlays. The same cull test as the base geometry applies; a stairs or trapdoor line in the
  block boundary is culled with that face and a line on an inner plane never, as vanilla does for the
  quads under it.
- If a glyph sprite is missing from the block atlas, no decal is drawn (never the missing-texture
  checkerboard).
- Feature OFF restores the unmodified active model; geometry keys still include the enabled mask.

## 4. Anvil arrow direction

The source pack keeps vanilla `template_anvil` and the vanilla blockstate rotations and replaces only
the `top` texture with an arrow that points toward -u. Its world direction is therefore decided by the
vanilla top-face UVs. Reading them locally was impossible (no vanilla assets in the development
environment), so the direction is fixed in `DecorationDecals.anvilArrow` (one counter-clockwise turn
from `facing`, which is the placer's look direction because vanilla turns the look clockwise into
`facing`) and checked where the vanilla model exists: `DecorationDecalOracle` emits the baked vanilla
anvil for each facing with every decoration off, takes the direction in which u decreases across its
single top quad, and requires the rendered decal to point the same way, on 26.1.2, 26.2 and 26.3. The
arrow is drawn only on quads in the block's top plane (`DecorationDecals.onBoundaryOnly`), so the base
and waist faces under the top carry nothing, as in the pack.

## 5. Stairs lines

The source pack draws stairs as a technical drawing in 32×32 per-material textures. BlockLens derives
the same lines from `facing`, `half` and `shape` (`DecorationLines`), with no source pixels:

- The shape is eight octants: the base half is full; the step half holds the back half (straight), the
  back half plus the front quarter on the left (inner-left) or right (inner-right) of `facing`, or
  only the back quarter on that side (outer-left, outer-right). "Left" is the same side for both
  halves, as in vanilla and the source pack.
- Each exposed surface (boundary planes and the planes through the middle) is a 2×2 grid of cells. On
  the back face's boundary plane every silhouette edge is green, and the inner corner of an L silhouette
  is closed. Any other face whose boundary plane is fully covered is a full face: amber outline, green
  where it meets the back face. Every other surface gets green only along an edge in the back boundary.
- On full faces, an edge between two cells is dashed when one has material behind it and the other does
  not: the step edges hidden behind the face. The dash runs inside the cell with nothing behind it.
- Lines are one sixteenth wide. They are rasterized per plane with green over amber over dashes and
  merged into rectangles, so no two quads overlap. A dashed run is one quad that samples the striped
  half of the line glyph with u along the run, one glyph width per block, so dashes sit on the odd
  sixteenths of the block whatever the run's length.

A straight stair draws 13 quads; inner shapes 20, outer shapes 17. A test fixture compared every one of
the 40 states, face by face, with the source pack's textures as mapped by its models: all lines agree
to within one sixteenth (the pack places some mid lines one pixel to either side). The source pack's
`south_top_outer_right` model references a missing texture on one face; BlockLens draws that state like
the other 39.

## 6. Trapdoor marks

The source pack replaces the trapdoor textures (16×16) with copies that add metal: two 2×1 hinge stubs at
3–5 and 11–13 sixteenths along the pivot edge and a 6×2 handle at 5–11 on the opposite edge of both
panel faces, and the same marks over the full 3/16 height of the hinge-side and handle-side edge faces.
`DecorationLines` draws exactly these, in one dark metal color (the source pack varies its metal per
wood). A closed trapdoor pivots on the edge opposite `facing`; an open one stands against that side and
pivots on its bottom edge (`half=bottom`) or top edge (`half=top`). The source pack has no dashed marks
on trapdoors.

## 7. Residual differences from the source pack

| Capability | Difference | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stairs | Flat green/amber instead of the source's shaded pixel colors; mid lines may sit one sixteenth to the other side of the middle. | Same lines, from clean-room geometry. |
| Trapdoors | One metal color for every material. | The decal is per (capability, state) and does not know the material; the marks and positions are the same. |
| Weighted/multipart or wrapped models | Tint fallback. | Their geometry can differ per block, so encoded decal quads could disagree with it. |

## 8. Verification

- `common`: `DecorationDecalsTest` (right-handed viewer frames, rotation math, source template rotation
  table for glazed terracotta, axis/slab semantics, packing and interning; mutation-gated),
  `DecorationLinesTest` (the drawings of straight, inner and outer stairs and of closed and open
  trapdoors, turning with `facing`, mirroring between left and right, flipping between halves, no
  overlapping lines, dashes only on full faces, vertex winding and lift, glyph coordinates;
  mutation-gated) and `DecorationDecalContractTest` (renderer wiring, glyph strip structure).
- `scripts/test_decoration_glyphs.py` and the CI clean-room step regenerate the strip bytes exactly.
- `DecorationDecalOracle` runs on 26.1.2, 26.2 and 26.3 clients: a top-down camera checks arrow direction
  for all four facings, rail orientation and legend color for all three axes, and that only top and
  double slabs mark their upper face. The arrow is read by matching the largest changed region against
  the shipped glyph in each rotation (IoU at least 0.6, margin 0.15); a mismatch logs the scores and a
  coarse mask, and the CI failure artifact keeps the `decoration-decals` captures. For anvils it also
  derives the source pack's arrow direction from the baked vanilla top quad (§4) for all four facings
  and records `pack=` and `decal=` per facing in the manifest. Upside-down straight stone stairs, seen
  from above for all four facings, must draw green toward `facing` and at least five dash runs across the
  middle; closed birch trapdoors facing north and east must carry the handle toward `facing` and two
  separate hinges on the opposite edge.
- Existing M3/M7/M5/terrain oracles keep their thresholds.
- The oracle adds about 55 s to each normal client run. 26.3 already measured 21m17s of the former
  22-minute step, so normal client steps get 27 minutes inside 36-minute jobs; no threshold changes.

Size: the decal layer is one table-driven class with no enum switch maps, and one sprite instead of seven,
to keep its artifact cost small. Decals add one cut-out quad per decorated face, and stairs and trapdoors
their 9 to 20 line quads, only while a decoration capability is enabled; their meshing cost against the source pack is measured by
[`pack-cost-comparison.md`](pack-cost-comparison.md).
